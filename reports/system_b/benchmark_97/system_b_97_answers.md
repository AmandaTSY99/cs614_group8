## gold-023

According to Apple's 2018 10-K, how do the specific risks associated with the company's retail store operations differ from the general risks associated with its international operations?

**System B answer**

Apple’s 2018 10‑K separates the risks that are unique to its retail stores from the broader risks tied to its worldwide business.

* **General international‑operation risks** – Apple notes that its overseas activities are exposed to “economic and labor conditions, increased duties, taxes and other costs, political instability and international trade disputes” and that “gross margins … could be materially adversely affected by international trade regulations, including duties, tariffs and antidumping penalties” as well as credit‑risk on trade receivables【2】.

* **Retail‑store‑specific risks** – The filing points out that Apple’s stores require “substantial investment in equipment and leasehold improvements, information systems, inventory and personnel” and that many stores are “high‑profile venues … that require substantially more investment than typical stores.” Because of this high‑cost structure, a decline in sales, closure or poor performance of one or more stores could trigger “significant lease termination costs, write‑offs of equipment and leasehold improvements and severance costs”【3】.

Thus, while the international risks focus on macro‑economic, regulatory and credit exposures across foreign markets, the retail‑store risks concentrate on the heavy capital commitments, lease obligations and potential losses that arise if individual stores under‑perform or are closed.

**Benchmark reference**

The risks associated with Apple's retail stores are primarily operational and financial, stemming from the high cost structure of equipment, leasehold improvements, and leases, which can lead to significant termination costs and write-offs if sales decline or stores underperform. In contrast, the risks associated with international operations are regulatory and geopolitical, involving compliance with complex foreign laws (such as data privacy and anti-corruption), exposure to trade disputes, tariffs, and political instability, which can affect gross margins and brand reputation.
